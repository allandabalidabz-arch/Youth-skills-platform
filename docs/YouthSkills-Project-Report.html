Abstract

The YouthSkills Program is a web-based digital-skills learning platform developed to address the challenge of accessible, structured digital education for young people. The system is open to any youth who can access a browser and register — it is not limited to students of any particular institution. Learners from anywhere can register using their National Registration Card (NRC) number, enrol in courses, study at their own pace, earn certificates, and download verified PDF certificates after completing a payment process.

The system was built using React 18 for the frontend, Node.js and Express for the backend REST API, and SQLite as the database. It provides three digital-skills courses — System Analysis and Design, Web Development, and Operating Systems — each containing three modules, one quiz per module, and one comprehensive final assignment per course.

The platform supports full learner workflows: registration with NRC validation, course enrolment, module study, quiz assessment (60% pass mark), assignment submission, progress tracking, certificate issuance, mobile money payment proof submission, and PDF certificate download. Administrators manage the platform through a dedicated portal where they can view registered youth, track completions, grade assignments, approve or reject payment proofs, and receive email notifications when payment is submitted.

The system was implemented as a prototype, tested locally, and all twenty-five test cases passed. It demonstrates that modern web technologies can deliver structured learning content, track learner progress, and issue verifiable digital certificates at low cost — serving as a scalable foundation for a nationwide youth digital empowerment initiative.

Keywords: digital skills, learning management system, youth empowerment, React, Node.js, SQLite, certificates, mobile money, Zambia

Chapter 1 — Introduction

1a. Introduction

The rapid advancement of information and communication technology has fundamentally changed how people learn, work, and participate in the economy. Digital skills — including knowledge of computing systems, web technologies, and software development — have become essential for young people seeking employment, self-employment, and participation in the digital economy. However, access to quality, structured digital education remains uneven, with many young people in developing countries, including Zambia, unable to access or afford formal technical training programmes.

This project proposes and implements the YouthSkills Program — a web-based digital-skills learning platform designed to provide structured, self-paced digital education to any youth who can access a browser and register, regardless of their location or institutional affiliation. The platform delivers three courses in system analysis, web development, and operating systems; tracks learner progress; issues verifiable digital certificates; and provides a full administration portal for managing the programme.

The system was developed as a final year Computer Science project at Evelyn Hone College, School of Business Studies and Management, Lusaka, Zambia.

1b. Background — Description of the Existing System

Prior to the development of the YouthSkills Program, digital skills training for youth was delivered through informal, unstructured methods. The existing approach typically involved the following:

  • Course materials distributed as printed handouts or shared informally through messaging applications such as WhatsApp.
  • Learner attendance and progress tracked manually in paper registers by facilitators.
  • Assessments conducted on paper and marked by hand, with no automated scoring or immediate feedback.
  • Certificates printed and signed manually, with no mechanism for independent public verification.
  • No centralised record of which youth had completed which course or earned which certificate.
  • Payment for certificate processing handled through informal cash transactions with no digital audit trail.
  • No means for a learner in a remote area to access course materials or submit work without physically attending a session.

These limitations made it difficult to scale the programme, reach youth in remote or rural areas, provide timely feedback, and give learners portable digital proof of their skills that employers and institutions could verify.

1c. Objectives

The objectives of the YouthSkills Program are as follows:

  1. To develop a web-based platform accessible to any youth with an internet connection, regardless of location or institutional affiliation.
  2. To implement NRC-validated registration ensuring every learner has a verified, unique identity in the system.
  3. To provide structured digital-skills courses with module content, multiple-choice quizzes (60% pass mark), and final assignments.
  4. To automatically issue digital certificates upon successful completion of all course modules.
  5. To implement a mobile money payment proof workflow enabling learners to pay for and download their certificates.
  6. To provide an administrator portal for managing youth accounts, viewing completions, grading assignments, and processing payments.
  7. To send email notifications to the administrator when payment proof is submitted.
  8. To enable public certificate verification by certificate number.

1d. Problem Statement

Young people seeking to develop digital skills face significant barriers including geographic distance from training centres, high cost of formal education, lack of portable proof of learning, and the absence of a scalable platform for structured self-paced digital education. The existing manual methods of delivering youth digital skills training are inefficient, difficult to scale beyond a single location, and do not provide learners with verifiable digital credentials that can be recognised by employers. A web-based learning platform is needed that is open to any youth, provides structured courses, tracks progress, and issues verifiable certificates — regardless of where the learner is located.

1e. Proposed System

The proposed YouthSkills Program is an open, browser-based learning platform with the following capabilities:

  • Open Registration: Any youth anywhere can register using their name, NRC number, email address, and password. The system validates the NRC format and ensures uniqueness. There is no geographic or institutional restriction on who can register.
  • Course Library: Three courses are available — System Analysis and Design, Web Development, and Operating Systems. Each course has three modules, one quiz per module, and one comprehensive final assignment.
  • Learning and Assessment: Learners study module content at their own pace, take multiple-choice quizzes (requiring 60% to pass), and submit a text-based final assignment for administrator grading.
  • Progress Tracking: A personal dashboard shows enrolled courses, completed modules, progress percentages, and earned certificates.
  • Certificate Issuance: A digital certificate with a unique number is automatically issued when all modules are completed. The PDF includes the learner's name, NRC number, course, date, instructor signature, and a public verification link.
  • Payment and Download: Learners submit mobile money payment proof (2,000 ZMW to number 0970342178). The administrator reviews and approves, unlocking the PDF download.
  • Admin Portal: Administrators access a six-tab portal covering Overview statistics, Registered Youth, Course Completions, Modules, Assignment Grading, and Payment Approval.

2.2 System 1 — Moodle Learning Management System

Overview

Moodle is a free, open-source Learning Management System first released in 2002 by Martin Dougiamas. It is used by universities, colleges, government institutions, and NGOs worldwide. In Zambia, Moodle is used by the University of Zambia (UNZA) and other institutions for online course delivery. The name Moodle stands for Modular Object-Oriented Dynamic Learning Environment (Moodle, n.d.).

Key Features

  • Course creation with text, video, file, and forum resources
  • Multiple assessment types: quizzes, assignments, peer review, and workshops
  • Learner progress tracking and grade book management
  • Multiple roles: student, teacher, administrator, course creator, and manager
  • Certificate plugins available as extensions
  • Email notifications for key events
  • Multi-language support and mobile app availability

Organisations Using the System

Moodle is used by over 400 million users across more than 240 countries. Notable Zambian users include the University of Zambia and various NGOs delivering remote education programmes (Moodle, n.d.).

Assessment

Moodle is a mature, feature-rich system well suited to large educational institutions with technical infrastructure. However, its complexity and dependency on PHP server environments make it difficult to deploy rapidly for a community programme. It lacks NRC validation and mobile money payment workflows specific to the Zambian context. YouthSkills Program provides a lightweight, purpose-built alternative that covers the specific workflow needed for the Youth Empowerment Programme.

2.3 System 2 — Google Classroom

Overview

Google Classroom is a free web service developed by Google and launched in 2014 as part of Google Workspace for Education. It is used extensively in secondary schools, colleges, and NGOs to distribute assignments, collect submissions, provide grades, and communicate with learners. Several schools in Lusaka use Google Classroom for blended and remote learning (Google, n.d.).

Key Features

  • Assignment creation and submission with due dates
  • Grading and written feedback through a built-in grade book
  • Class announcements and direct messaging
  • Integration with Google Drive, Docs, Slides, and Meet
  • Basic learner progress overview for teachers
  • Mobile application available for Android and iOS

Organisations Using the System

Google Classroom is used by more than 170 million students and educators worldwide (Google, n.d.). In Zambia it is used by various schools and learning institutions for assignment management and communication.

Assessment

Google Classroom is easy to use and widely accessible but requires a Google account and constant internet connectivity. It does not provide built-in quiz scoring, certificate issuance, or a payment workflow. It is primarily a communication and assignment-sharing tool rather than a self-contained learning management system. The YouthSkills Program provides a more complete end-to-end experience that does not depend on third-party services.

2.4 Summary

Both Moodle and Google Classroom demonstrate that web-based learning platforms can improve the delivery of education and reduce manual administrative work. However, neither addresses the specific requirements of the YouthSkills Programme: open access for any youth regardless of institution, NRC-based identity verification, mobile money payment proof submission, lightweight local deployment, and a built-in certificate issuance and administration workflow. The YouthSkills Program fills this gap by combining the core functions of a learning management system with features specifically designed for the Zambian youth empowerment context.

Chapter 3 — Requirements Specification / Success Criteria

3.1 Introduction

This chapter describes the functional and non-functional requirements of the YouthSkills Program, the user roles and responsibilities, the fact-finding methods used, and the success criteria against which the system is evaluated.

3.2 Fact-Finding Methods

The following methods were used to gather requirements for the system:

a) Interviews

Informal interviews were conducted with prospective youth learners and a programme coordinator to understand what features would be most useful, what barriers existed to accessing training, and what information the system needed to capture about each learner. Interviews revealed that learners wanted immediate feedback on quizzes, the ability to study at their own pace from home, and a portable digital certificate they could share with employers.

b) Observation

The manual process of registering learners, distributing materials, recording attendance, and issuing certificates was observed. This identified specific inefficiencies: paper registers were difficult to search, certificates had no verification mechanism, and there was no way for facilitators to know which learner was progressing and which had stalled.

c) Document Analysis

Existing programme materials — course outlines, attendance registers, and sample certificates — were reviewed. This informed the design of the course and module structure, the fields required on the certificate, and the layout of the PDF template.

4.2 Application Design

4.2.1 System Architecture

The YouthSkills Program follows a three-tier client/server architecture:

  1. Presentation Tier — React 18 single-page application served by Vite on port 3000. Handles all UI rendering, routing, and user interaction.
  2. Application Tier — Node.js and Express REST API on port 5000. Contains all business logic, authentication, authorisation, and data access.
  3. Data Tier — SQLite database stored as a local file (database.sqlite). Accessed exclusively by the API layer.

4.3 User Interface Design

4.3.1 Design Principles

The user interface was designed following five core principles:

  1. Consistency — the same layout, colour scheme, typography, and component styles are used on every page.
  2. Simplicity — only the controls relevant to the current action are visible at any given time.
  3. Feedback — all user actions trigger toast notifications confirming success or reporting errors.
  4. Accessibility — semantic HTML elements, labelled form inputs, and sufficient colour contrast are used throughout.
  5. Responsiveness — the layout adapts correctly to tablet and desktop screen sizes using Tailwind CSS utility classes.

  • Background: Slate-50 (light mode), Slate-900 (dark mode)
  • Font: Inter (Google Fonts), with system-ui and serif fallbacks
  • Dark mode: Toggled by user; preference stored in localStorage

4.4 Security Design

4.4.1 Authentication

All passwords are hashed using bcryptjs with a cost factor of 12 before storage. Plaintext passwords are never stored or returned through any API response. JWT tokens are signed using a secret key stored in the backend environment configuration file (.env). Tokens expire after 7 days. The authenticate middleware verifies every token on protected routes, reloads the user from the database, and attaches the user object to the request context.

4.4.2 Authorisation

Role-based access control is enforced on every protected route. The authorize middleware checks the loaded user's role against the permitted roles. Admin-only routes — grading, payment approval, user listing, dashboard statistics — return HTTP 403 Forbidden if accessed by non-admin accounts. The regular youth login page actively blocks admin accounts and redirects them to the dedicated admin login URL, separating the two login flows.

4.4.3 Input Validation and SQL Injection Prevention

All form inputs are validated on the frontend using regular expressions and required-field checks, providing inline error messages before any API request is made. The same validation rules are applied independently on the backend before any database write occurs. This dual-layer approach prevents invalid data from entering the system even if the frontend is bypassed. All database queries use parameterised statements provided by the sqlite package, which prevents SQL injection attacks by treating all user-supplied values as data rather than executable SQL.

4.4.4 Data Protection

The backend .env file containing the JWT secret, Gmail credentials, and other sensitive configuration values is excluded from version control using .gitignore. The SQLite database file is stored locally and is not exposed through any API endpoint. The CORS policy on the Express server restricts browser requests to the configured frontend origin only, preventing requests from unauthorised domains.

Chapter 6 — Implementation

6.1 Introduction

This chapter describes how the YouthSkills Program was implemented, the tools and technologies used, the challenges encountered during development, and suggestions for wider future deployment.

6.2 How the System Was Implemented

The YouthSkills Program was implemented as a locally hosted prototype running on a single Windows 11 development machine. The implementation followed a structured sequence of steps:

Step 1 — Environment Setup

Node.js LTS and npm were installed. Visual Studio Code was used as the integrated development environment. Git was configured for version control, with the repository hosted on GitHub at https://github.com/allandabalidabz-arch/Youth-skills-platform.

Step 2 — Database Design and Backend API

The SQLite database schema was designed first, covering all twelve tables with foreign keys and cascade rules. The Express backend was built with separate route files for each functional area (auth, users, courses, progress, assignments, certificates, payments, notifications, dashboard). JWT authentication middleware was implemented as shared middleware applied to all protected routes. Nodemailer was configured to send email notifications through Gmail SMTP when learners submit payment proof.

Step 3 — Seed Data

A seed script was written to populate the database with one administrator account, three complete courses, nine modules, nine quizzes, and three final assignments. The seed script is triggered automatically when the backend starts and detects an empty database, ensuring the system is ready for use immediately after installation.

Step 4 — Frontend Development

The React 18 frontend was built using Vite as the build and development tool and Tailwind CSS for styling. React Router v6 was used for client-side navigation with protected route wrappers that redirect unauthenticated users to the login page. Axios was configured with interceptors to attach the JWT token to every request and to redirect to the login page when a 401 Unauthorized response is received. All pages were built as functional components using React hooks.

Step 5 — Integration and Testing

The Vite development server was configured to proxy all /api requests to the Express backend on port 5000. This allowed seamless frontend-backend communication during development. Manual testing was conducted across all features following the test plan in Chapter 5. Issues found during testing were corrected before the final build.

Step 6 — Documentation

All documentation was written following the Evelyn Hone College final year project guidelines. The system was version-controlled throughout, with each major feature committed to GitHub with a descriptive message. The final codebase is publicly accessible on GitHub.

6.3 Implemented Features Summary

  • Open youth registration with NRC validation — any youth anywhere can register
  • JWT authentication and role-based access control (youth and admin)
  • Three complete courses: System Analysis and Design, Web Development, Operating Systems
  • Nine modules with full text content, one quiz per module, one final assignment per course
  • Module completion, quiz scoring (60% pass mark), and course progress tracking
  • Automatic certificate issuance on course completion with redirect
  • Certificate PDF generation (light green design, single logo, NRC on certificate)
  • Mobile money payment proof submission (to 0970342178 — Allan Dabali)
  • Admin approval/rejection of payments with optional reject reason
  • Email notification to admin on payment submission (via Nodemailer/Gmail)
  • Admin dashboard with six tabs: Overview, Registered Youth, Completions, Modules, Assignments, Payments
  • Public certificate verification by certificate number
  • Dark mode, in-app notifications, and full profile management including NRC

6.4 Challenges Encountered

  • Stale JWT tokens: After database reseeding, existing browser tokens referenced deleted user IDs, causing 403 errors on the dashboard. This was resolved by removing the strict authorize middleware on the youth dashboard route and improving the error handling to show a "Try Again" button instead of a blank page.
  • Assignment status display: The assignment form showed "Resubmit" immediately after a first submission, which confused learners. This was fixed by showing an "Awaiting Grading" notice after submission instead of the input form.
  • NRC field migration: Adding the NRC field to the existing database required an auto-migration step to alter the users table on backend startup without requiring a full reseed.
  • Email configuration: Gmail's security model requires an App Password rather than an account password. Clear instructions were added to the documentation and .env file to guide setup.

6.5 Suggestions for Future Implementation

  • Cloud deployment: Deploy the backend to a cloud server (Railway, Render, or a VPS) and the frontend to Vercel or Netlify, making the platform accessible to youth anywhere with internet access.
  • Email password reset: Integrate a full email delivery service so password reset links are sent directly to learners, replacing the current console-log approach.
  • Server-side quiz scoring: Move quiz answer keys to the server exclusively and calculate all scores server-side to prevent any possibility of score manipulation.
  • PostgreSQL database: Replace SQLite with PostgreSQL for concurrent multi-user production use and easier cloud hosting.
  • File attachments for assignments: Implement file upload support using the multer package (already installed) to allow learners to submit diagrams and documents alongside their text responses.
  • Course authoring interface: Build an admin screen for creating and editing courses, modules, and quizzes without requiring developer access to the database or seed file.
  • Mobile application: Develop a Progressive Web App or React Native version for learners who primarily access the internet via mobile phone.
  • Integration with national systems: Explore integration with TEVETA (Technical Education, Vocational and Entrepreneurship Training Authority) or ZQA (Zambia Qualifications Authority) to give YouthSkills certificates formal national recognition.

Chapter 7 — Conclusion and Recommendations

7.1 Conclusion

The YouthSkills Program has been successfully designed, implemented, and tested as a web-based digital-skills learning platform for youth empowerment. The system is open to any youth who can access a browser and register — there is no requirement to be affiliated with Evelyn Hone College or any other institution. A learner in Lusaka, Ndola, Livingstone, or any rural area with internet access can register, study at their own pace, earn a certificate, and submit payment proof for a verified digital credential they can share with employers.

All twenty functional requirements were implemented. All twenty-five test cases passed without critical failure. The platform supports the complete learner journey from open registration through course completion, certificate issuance, payment proof submission, and PDF download. The administrator portal provides comprehensive visibility over registered youth, course completions, assignment submissions, and payment records — with email alerts on new payment submissions.

The system demonstrates that modern web technologies — React 18, Node.js, Express, and SQLite — can be combined to build a functional, secure, and visually polished digital learning platform at very low cost, using entirely free and open-source tools. It serves as both a working prototype and a strong foundation for a scalable national youth digital empowerment initiative.

7.2 Recommendations

  1. Conduct user acceptance testing with a representative group of real youth learners from different locations to gather usability feedback before any formal launch.
  2. Deploy to a cloud server so that any youth with an internet connection can access the platform without needing a local installation.
  3. Implement server-side quiz scoring before using the certificates for any consequential purpose, to ensure assessment integrity.
  4. Add email delivery for password resets to make the password recovery process fully self-service for learners in remote areas.
  5. Expand the course catalogue with additional courses in networking, cybersecurity, data analysis, and digital entrepreneurship as the programme grows.
  6. Seek formal recognition from TEVETA or ZQA so that YouthSkills certificates carry formal national weight for employment and further education applications.
  7. Add automated tests using Jest and React Testing Library to ensure system reliability is maintained as the platform evolves.
  8. Consider mobile-first development given that many Zambian youth primarily access the internet via mobile phone rather than desktop or laptop.

Appendix B — User Manual

B.1 For Youth Learners

The YouthSkills Program is open to any youth who can access a browser and register. You do not need to be a student at any particular school or institution. If you have an internet connection and a valid Zambian NRC, you can join.

B.1.1 Creating an Account

  1. Open the application in your browser and click Get Started Free
  2. Fill in the registration form:
    • Full Name — letters only (no numbers or symbols)
    • NRC Number — required; format: 123456/78/9
    • Email Address — required; must be a valid email
    • Location — optional (your city and country)
    • Phone — optional
    • Password — minimum 6 characters
    • Confirm Password — must match the password above
  3. Click Create Account
  4. You will be taken directly to your personal dashboard

B.1.2 Signing In

  1. Go to the login page
  2. Enter your registered email address and password
  3. Tick Remember me if you want to stay signed in after closing the browser
  4. Click Sign In

B.1.3 Your Dashboard

Your dashboard shows your enrolled courses with progress bars, the number of certificates earned, your overall completion rate, and recent certificates. If you have not enrolled in any courses yet, a Browse Courses button guides you to the course library.

B.1.4 Enrolling in a Course

  1. Click Courses in the left sidebar
  2. Browse or search for a course using the filters
  3. Click on a course card to open its detail page
  4. Click Enroll Now — Free
  5. The modules will unlock and your progress bar will appear

B.1.5 Studying a Module and Taking a Quiz

  1. Open an enrolled course and click on a module to expand it
  2. Read all the module content carefully
  3. When ready, click Take Quiz
  4. Select one answer for each question
  5. Click Submit Quiz
  6. Your score is shown immediately. You need at least 60% to pass
  7. If you score below 60%, click Retry Quiz and try again
  8. On passing, the module is marked complete automatically

B.1.6 Submitting an Assignment

  1. Open any module in the course — the assignment section appears at the bottom
  2. Read the assignment title, description, and full instructions carefully
  3. Type your response in the text box (minimum 20 characters)
  4. Click Submit Assignment
  5. The status changes to ⏳ Awaiting Grading
  6. Once graded, you will receive a notification with your score and feedback

B.1.7 Earning and Downloading Your Certificate

  1. Complete all three modules in a course (pass all three quizzes)
  2. The system will automatically redirect you to the Certificates page
  3. Your certificate card will appear — click the 2,000 ZMW button
  4. A payment panel opens showing the mobile money number: 0970 342 178 — Allan Dabali, YouthSkills Program
  5. Send 2,000 ZMW via mobile money to that number
  6. Enter your transaction reference number in the input field
  7. Click Submit Payment Proof
  8. Status changes to Pending
  9. Once the administrator approves, you receive a notification
  10. Click the PDF button to download your certificate

Your certificate includes your full name, NRC number, course title, date of completion, certificate number, instructor name, and a public verification link.

B.1.8 Sharing and Verifying Your Certificate

  • Click Share on your certificate card to copy a public verification link
  • Anyone — including employers — can visit that link to confirm your certificate is genuine
  • No login is required to verify a certificate

B.1.9 Updating Your Profile

  1. Click Profile in the sidebar
  2. Update your name, bio, location, phone, NRC, or avatar image URL
  3. Add or remove skills using the Skills section
  4. Click Save Changes
  5. Use the Security tab to change your password

  2. Enter: admin@youthskills.com / Allan7034
  3. You will be taken to the Admin Dashboard
